\begin{abstract}
  Geospatial foundation models produce planet-scale \emph{embedding lakes}---large, centralized stores of precomputed vectors analogous to data lakes---whose storage and retrieval costs dominate deployment budgets.
  Recent systems distribute global embedding fields at \texttt{int8} precision, but the quality--cost trade-off below 8 bits remains unexplored for remote sensing embeddings.
  We present \method{}, a post-hoc quantization study spanning \texttt{float16} through 1-bit \texttt{binary} on \num{49785116} real 1024D embeddings (\textit{183\,GiB}), evaluated with label-free neighborhood-preservation metrics grounded in intrinsic-dimension analysis.
  \texttt{Int4} retains 91\% kNN recall at $6.3\times$ compression; \texttt{binary} achieves $16.5\times$ compression with 65\% recall, sufficient for coarse-stage candidate generation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Geospatial foundation models~\citep{mendieta2023gfm,jakubik2024prithvi,clay2025model} produce high-dimensional embeddings for retrieval, change detection, deduplication, and spatial indexing, forming \emph{embedding lakes}---large, centralized vector stores analogous to data lakes.
At planet scale, storage and memory bandwidth---not model accuracy---become the deployment bottleneck.
A corpus of 50M embeddings at $d{=}1024$ in \texttt{float32} occupies \textit{183\,GiB}; halving per-vector cost saves nearly \textit{100\,GiB} with no retraining.

Two recent systems highlight this tension.
AlphaEarth releases global embedding fields cast to \texttt{int8} via post-hoc calibration~\citep{brown2025alphaearth}.
TESSERA applies quantization-aware training (QAT) to a learned 128D bottleneck and distributes the result at \texttt{int8}~\citep{feng2025tessera}.
Both treat \texttt{int8} as the practical floor.
\method{} asks: \textit{how far below \texttt{int8} can we push post-hoc quantization while preserving nearest-neighbor structure?}

Figure~\ref{fig:pareto} previews the answer.
We sweep seven bit-widths from \texttt{float16} to 1-bit \texttt{binary} on a real 49.8M-embedding corpus and measure neighborhood preservation with label-free metrics.
The frontier is smooth: \texttt{int4} retains 91\% kNN recall at $6.3\times$ compression, while \texttt{binary} reaches $16.5\times$ with recall sufficient for shortlist retrieval~\citep{wang2018binary}.

Our contributions are:
(1)~an intrinsic-dimension-guided evaluation framework combining ID diagnostics~\citep{levina2004id,facco2017twonn} with label-free retrieval fidelity metrics;
(2)~a systematic post-hoc quantization study (\texttt{float16}, \texttt{fp8}, \texttt{int8}, \texttt{int4}, \texttt{int3}, \texttt{int2}, \texttt{binary}) on 49.8M real geospatial embeddings;
(3)~a schema-preserving parallel Parquet transformation pipeline that processes the full corpus in 45\,min at \num{18359}\,rows/s; and
(4)~a retrieval benchmark showing $>$\num{1300}\,QPS on GPU with recall preserved across hardware.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Geospatial foundation models.}
Recent work trains large vision encoders on satellite imagery and releases pretrained embeddings as data products~\citep{mendieta2023gfm,jakubik2024prithvi,clay2025model}.
AlphaEarth~\citep{brown2025alphaearth} and TESSERA~\citep{feng2025tessera} both distribute global \texttt{int8} embedding fields, recognizing that storage cost matters as much as model quality at deployment scale.

\paragraph{Vector quantization and retrieval.}
Product quantization~\citep{jegou2011pq}, learned anisotropic quantization~\citep{guo2020scann}, and locality-sensitive hashing~\citep{indyk1998lsh} compress embeddings for approximate nearest-neighbor search.
FAISS~\citep{johnson2021faiss} implements these at billion scale.
LLM.\texttt{int8}()~\citep{dettmers2022int8} demonstrates that aggressive quantization preserves model quality in language; Matryoshka representations~\citep{kusupati2022matryoshka} show that embedding dimensions can be truncated with graceful degradation.

\paragraph{Positioning.}
Prior vector-compression work targets web-scale text/image embeddings or model-weight quantization.
\method{} focuses on the less-studied regime of post-hoc quantization of geospatial embedding lakes, where spatial metadata must be preserved alongside vectors, and label-free evaluation is the practical reality.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Problem setup.}

Given a corpus $X = \{x_i\}_{i=1}^N$, $x_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$, stored in partitioned Parquet files with spatial/temporal metadata columns, we apply a quantizer $\hat{x}_i = \mathcal{Q}_m(x_i)$ under method $m$ and evaluate each method by storage footprint, neighborhood preservation, and end-to-end throughput.
Our corpus comprises \num{49785116} embeddings ($d{=}1024$) produced by the Clay v1.5 foundation model~\citep{clay2025model}, a ViT trained via masked autoencoding on multi-sensor satellite imagery.

\paragraph{Intrinsic-dimension diagnostics.}

Before compression, we estimate the intrinsic dimension (ID) of the embedding manifold using MLE~\citep{levina2004id}, TwoNN~\citep{facco2017twonn}, and local PCA participation ratio across preprocessing variants (raw, centered, L2-normalized).
ID estimates guide expectations for viable compression depth: if the data manifold has effective dimension $\hat{d} \ll d$, aggressive quantization should not collapse local neighborhood structure.

\paragraph{Quantization methods.}

We evaluate seven post-hoc methods spanning 16 bits to 1 bit:
\texttt{float16} (IEEE half-precision truncation);
\texttt{fp8}~E4M3 (8-bit floating point with 4-bit exponent);
\texttt{int}$b$ for $b \in \{8,4,3,2\}$ (per-dimension affine quantization with stored scale/zero-point vectors, followed by fixed-width bit packing for $b < 8$); and
\texttt{binary} (sign-bit encoding, $\operatorname{sign}(x_i) \in \{0,1\}^d$, packed to $d/8$ bytes, supporting Hamming-distance search~\citep{wang2018binary}).

\paragraph{Evaluation metrics.}
\label{sec:metrics}

We report label-free, retrieval-oriented metrics:
\textbf{kNN recall@$k$} ($k \in \{10,25,50\}$) under cosine and Euclidean distance, measuring the fraction of true $k$-nearest neighbors preserved after quantization;
\textbf{cosine correlation}, the Spearman rank correlation of pairwise cosine similarities (original vs.\ quantized); and
\textbf{reconstruction cosine similarity} and MSE (when dequantization is available).

\paragraph{Transformation pipeline.}

The pipeline processes Hive-partitioned Parquet files in parallel.
For each file, it reads the embedding column, applies quantizers, replaces only that column, and writes output preserving directory structure, non-embedding columns, and schema.
Quantization parameters (scale, zero-point, method, bit-width) are stored in Parquet footer metadata for downstream decompression.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Dataset.}

We evaluate on \num{49785116} embeddings ($d{=}1024$, \texttt{float32}) from a geospatial foundation model, stored across \num{1021} Parquet files totaling \textit{183\,GiB}.
Files are Hive-partitioned by level-2 geohash (\num{511} cells), year, and month.
Each record carries spatial metadata (geohash, bounding box, WKB geometry), temporal metadata, and provenance identifiers alongside the embedding vector.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
We evaluate along two axes: quality and systems efficiency.
For quality, we compute label-free fidelity metrics on a \num{20000}-row reservoir sample (reconstruction metrics are exact over all \num{49785116} rows).
For systems, we run the full-corpus transformation over all 7 methods with 8 parallel workers, reporting wall-clock time and I/O throughput.

ID estimation on a \num{10000}-sample subset yields MLE $\hat{d} = 17.0$, TwoNN $\hat{d} = 12.6$, and LPCA $\hat{d} = 17.0$ (Figure~\ref{fig:id}), confirming substantial structural redundancy relative to ambient dimension $d{=}1024$.

\paragraph{Quality--compression frontier.}

Table~\ref{tab:quality} summarizes quality across all methods.
\texttt{Int8} yields near-lossless retrieval (recall@10 = 0.99) at $3.5\times$ compression, matching the precision used by AlphaEarth and TESSERA~\citep{brown2025alphaearth,feng2025tessera}.
\texttt{Int4} provides a strong balanced operating point: $6.3\times$ compression with 91\% kNN recall.
\texttt{Binary} achieves the highest compression ($16.5\times$) with recall@10 of 0.65---suitable as a coarse first-pass filter before re-ranking with higher-fidelity representations~\citep{wang2018binary,jegou2011pq}.

\paragraph{Discussion.}

Three findings emerge.
First, ID estimates of 13--20 (vs.\ ambient $d{=}1024$) explain why aggressive quantization does not immediately collapse neighborhood structure: most per-dimension variance is redundant for local geometry.
Second, the quality decline from \texttt{int8} to \texttt{int2} is gradual (Figure~\ref{fig:knn-k}), enabling practitioners to select operating points based on their recall requirements versus storage budget.
Third, \texttt{binary} quantization is not quality-preserving in a strict sense, but its $16.5\times$ compression and compatibility with Hamming-distance search make it attractive for first-pass candidate generation in multi-stage retrieval pipelines~\citep{wang2018binary}.

\paragraph{Retrieval benchmark.}

To validate that quantization-induced compression translates to practical search speedups, we benchmark brute-force kNN retrieval on a 1M-vector subset (\num{1000} queries, $k{=}50$) using FAISS~\citep{johnson2021faiss} on CPU and PyTorch \texttt{cdist} on a single NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU.
Each quantized corpus is dequantized to \texttt{float32} before indexing, isolating the effect of quantization noise from format-specific index optimizations; binary embeddings use Hamming distance directly via packed \texttt{uint8} search.

Table~\ref{tab:search} and Figure~\ref{fig:search} summarize throughput and recall.
GPU search achieves \num{1322}--\num{1362}\,QPS for dequantized methods---a $22\times$ speedup over FAISS CPU (\num{59}--\num{60}\,QPS)---with identical recall, confirming that quantization quality is hardware-independent.
Binary Hamming search runs at \num{801}\,QPS on CPU and \num{164}\,QPS on GPU; the lower GPU throughput reflects the overhead of bit-unpacking binary vectors into float tensors for \texttt{cdist}, a limitation of the brute-force approach that purpose-built Hamming kernels would eliminate.

\paragraph{Limitations.}

We use label-free neighborhood-preservation proxies rather than task-specific metrics (e.g., land-cover classification accuracy).
kNN recall and cosine correlation are computed on a reservoir sample, not the full corpus.
Our retrieval benchmark uses brute-force search; approximate nearest-neighbor indices (IVF, HNSW) may alter the quality--throughput frontier at larger scale~\citep{johnson2021faiss}.
Future work should add labeled downstream evaluation and investigate ANN index interactions with low-bit quantization.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\method{} provides a systematic compression study for geospatial embedding lakes.
On 49.8M real embeddings, low-bit quantization yields a smooth quality--cost frontier: \texttt{int4}/\texttt{int8} are strong balanced choices; \texttt{binary} is viable for coarse retrieval stages.
GPU-accelerated brute-force search over dequantized embeddings exceeds \num{1300}\,QPS on a single RTX 3090, demonstrating that quantized embedding lakes are practical for real-time retrieval at scale.
